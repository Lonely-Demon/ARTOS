# Referenced Task Review: Using Existing Framework for Upcoming Task

**Referenced task:** `80HbnErd57jvZt90n0TbkD`

**Review status:** Complete review of the 142-event conversation and five attached artefacts.

## What the referenced task was supposed to do

The user asked the assistant to use the Universal Enterprise framework and pipeline from the referenced work for the upcoming IDREA robotics challenge. The user explicitly required an active continuity state, no premature conclusions, upstream research, problem decomposition, landscape mapping, and a concept that was more rigorous than a Round 1 pitch because later prototype funding might become available.

The immediate competition artefacts were a maximum ten-content-slide concept presentation and a user-stated maximum two-page PDF abstract. The official rulebook confirms the ten-slide Round 1 presentation; the abstract requirement remains a user-stated constraint pending official re-verification.

## Failure sequence observed

The task violated the requested operating model in three connected ways.

First, it jumped from a shallow reading of the rulebook to proposing adhesion mechanisms such as vortex or active suction, dry adhesives, and articulated locomotion. No problem reconstruction, decision-space map, factor catalogue, or evidence threshold existed at that point.

Second, it introduced claims about industrial inspection workflows, rope access, scaffolding, existing robotic alternatives, surface conditions, and failure consequences as though they were established facts. The user correctly objected that these were unsupported conclusions and explicitly restated that thorough research is mandatory.

Third, after reading the Mars rover example, it still jumped into cross-domain dependencies and mechanism families before returning to the first required sequence step: understanding the given problem. The user explicitly stated that the robotics challenge should be approached on the same playing field as the rover example and criticised the sequencing.

The inherited task later produced a preliminary factor catalogue and three candidate architecture families. These are useful historical work products, but they are not a selected architecture and should not be treated as final evidence.

## Corrected interpretation

The supplied challenge must be preserved as the starting problem. The enterprise should not replace “design this robotic prototype” with a different problem. It should accept the task, decompose it into the mission, operating concept, requirements, capabilities, dependencies, alternatives, and evidence needs, and also ask the higher-level questions needed to understand whether the supplied task is coherent and what it is intended to demonstrate.

The correct order is:

1. Frame the supplied challenge and its deliverables.
2. Establish the literal official fact base.
3. Reconstruct the real problem and operating context using research.
4. Map the problem, operating, technical, solution, evaluation, regulatory, and industrial landscapes.
5. Build the factor, evidence, and dependency catalogue.
6. Derive requirements, success conditions, verification methods, and exclusions.
7. Generate and compare architecture families.
8. Record the decision, alternatives, dissent, assumptions, consequences, and revisit triggers.
9. Produce the canonical technical concept.
10. Attack, calculate, simulate, test, and validate the concept at the claimed maturity.
11. Compress the canonical work into the abstract and presentation.

## Non-canonical content that remains quarantined

The following are not rejected forever; they are simply not authorised as decisions:

- Suction-and-airflow hybrid adhesion on an articulated chassis.
- Multi-modal magnetic plus dry-adhesive or spine-based adhesion.
- Soft/reconfigurable conforming robotics.
- Claims about current inspection practice not yet re-researched in the present canonical project.
- Claims that mathematical plausibility or a conceptual diagram constitute real-world validation.

## Immediate next action

Perform evidence-backed problem and operating-landscape research. The first research output should identify what is known, what is unknown, what is disputed, which environmental and surface factors are architecture-changing, and which claims can be supported by official, academic, industrial, or measured evidence. It should not recommend a mechanism merely because the mechanism appears technically interesting.
