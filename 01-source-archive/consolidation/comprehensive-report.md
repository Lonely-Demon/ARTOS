# Consolidated Reasoning Model — Comprehensive Report

*Reconciling the blind-derived final snapshot (nine conversation exports, `snapshots/snapshot-after-file-9.md`) against the conversation-derived model (self-report plus an earlier partial reading), per the Consolidation Protocol.*

## 1. How to read this report

This report reconciles two independently-built models of the same person: a **blind-derived model** (nine conversation exports, read one at a time by an extraction process with no access to self-report data or to any conclusion from a prior reading — only the category definitions and the evidence discipline) and a **conversation-derived model** (this person's own direct statements about how they think, plus an earlier partial reading of some of the same material). Where they agree despite being built from different evidence with no shared access, that agreement is real signal. Where they diverge, the divergence is handled by the specific reconciliation rules in the Consolidation Protocol, not smoothed over.

Four tags recur throughout:
- **Evidence tag** — *observation* (a direct reading of quoted evidence) vs. *inference* (a pattern synthesized across more than one point). A separate axis from *self-report vs. file-behavior*: self-report is this person's own account of their process, quoted in full where available; file-behavior is what was independently seen them do. Self-report is real evidence, but weighted as the weaker of the two — people are not always reliable narrators of their own reasoning, even when sincere.
- **Confidence** — low / moderate / high, reflecting the clarity and number of independent instances, not how interesting a finding feels.
- **Tier** — the finding's status as a candidate for standing operational use: *Established* (standing pattern, ideally corroborated across independent contexts, with a clear policy or goal attached), *Gated — conditional-trigger* (real, but only relevant when a specific condition is detected), *Gated — goal-anchored* (thin evidence, but grounds an independently-justified goal so its policy runs regardless), *Conditional* (real and worth keeping, but no policy attached yet and evidence stays thin), or *Special case* (the care-override instance, never tiered like an ordinary finding).
- **Process position** — where in this person's reasoning arc, in their own observable terms, a behavior tends to occur — never mapped onto an imported stage template.

One honest caveat up front, stated once here rather than repeated at every entry: I did not have the category-space artifact's internal `D`-numbered item names when the blind extraction ran — nor should I have, since that traceability existed specifically to keep the blind reads uncontaminated. I now have it for this consolidation step, and its Section 3 sensitivity ratings and Section 2 clarifications (on D2, D10, D19, D21, and the interaction hypotheses) are used below exactly as intended: only to interpret this reconciliation, never to have shaped what was searched for.

## 4. New candidates surfaced

The category-space artifact names four previously-flagged-but-not-adopted candidate dimensions: **Problem framing**, **Learning style**, **Communication style**, and **Error-pattern detection**. Checking the blind model's nine uncategorized entries against these:

- **Problem framing** — also a clean hit, on reflection more precise than my first pass at this mapping. `uncategorized-05`'s core finding (dense personal-context front-loading blended directly into the *very first* statement of a new own-venture project, before any decomposition begins) matches this dimension's definition — "how the actual problem, question, or goal gets defined and initially stated, before any decomposition or solution work begins" — more precisely than it matches Communication Style.

This absence isn't on the pre-registered sensitivity map (that map covers only the six Conditional-tier D-items), so there's no principled way to calibrate how meaningful it is the way curiosity-direction's absence-that-wasn't could be — it can only be reported that these two didn't come up, without a principled read on whether that's because the material doesn't contain them or because nothing prompted them to surface.

Both are stated here as candidates for a decision, exactly as the protocol asks — not silently folded into an existing category, not silently added as new ones.

## 5. Contradictions found and how they were resolved

**Confidence calibration — is it deadline-gated?** The conversation-derived model tiered this Gated (conditional-trigger), specifically on the strength of one instance that happened to occur under deadline pressure. The blind pass's much fuller read shows the same disposition (honest, well-calibrated domain-skill disclosure) recurring across contexts with no deadline pressure at all — e.g., a flat ML-skill self-rating volunteered in response to a direct question, mid-planning, with no deadline in play. This is classified as a **mode-explained differential**: the difference is fully accounted for by the earlier reading having seen one instance, versus the blind pass's much more complete sampling. Per the reconciliation rule, the blind pass's finding is adopted going forward — the pattern is general, not deadline-gated — and the item is promoted from Gated to Established accordingly.

**Attention allocation — is logistics really "later"?** The conversation-derived model states the pattern as "logistics- and communication-related needs receiving attention later than technical ones." The blind pass's clearest instance directly shows the opposite ordering when logistics had the nearer deadline (a PPT review deadline preceded, and was handled before, any code work in the same project). The real mechanism is deadline-proximity-driven, not task-type-driven, and the earlier partial reading's single instance happened to occur when technical work had the nearer deadline — no instance was found anywhere of technical work being deferred despite having the nearer deadline, so there's no genuine second branch to preserve, just a narrower framing fully explained by a broader one.

*Methodological note, flagged rather than quietly applied:* the word used for this resolution in the first version of this report was "superseded," borrowed from the protocol's temporal-evolution clause — but that clause specifically means the person's approach *changing over time*, which isn't what happened here; there's no time-separation story, just a broader mechanism that fully accounts for the narrower one from the moment both were true. This resolution doesn't cleanly match any of the reconciliation method's named outcomes: not "mode-explained differential" (defined as a *confidence* difference from a fuller read, not a *framing* correction); not a moderating-condition branch (nothing survives as a distinct, condition-tied claim); not mixed-evidence (nothing was left unresolved); not temporal supersession (nothing changed over time). What actually happened is a fourth pattern the protocol doesn't name: **generalization without a residual branch** — a narrower, earlier finding turns out, once fuller evidence is available, to be fully subsumed by a broader mechanism, with no distinct behavior left over to preserve as its own branch. The call itself holds up; the vocabulary borrowed to describe it didn't fit, and that gap should be added to the reconciliation method explicitly if this pipeline runs again, rather than leaving each future run to reinvent an unnamed exception.

**Verification timing — does rigor lapse under time pressure, or hold?** This one required the most care. Read literally, the conversation-derived model's D15 ("treating something as complete before it was actually confirmed, occurring under acute time pressure specifically") describes a *lapse* — his usual high verification rigor failing under pressure. The blind pass's most extreme test of this exact condition shows the opposite: verification-seeking survived intact even in the last ninety seconds before a hard, binary deadline, at the real cost of missing that deadline. Taken at face value, that's a direct opposition. But the blind pass, working completely independently, had *already* identified a plausible moderating condition on its own: a distinct, separately-evidenced split between technical/deliverable claims (extremely high rigor, doesn't degrade even under extreme pressure) and personal/logistical facts (materially weaker rigor — an unverified date assumption caused a real, multi-hour scheduling failure). D15's original single instance is most plausibly read as falling into this second, weaker branch rather than contradicting the first. This is recorded as **two branches of one item, not a contradiction** — and it's worth stating plainly that this is a case where two independent processes converged on the *same distinguishing condition* (claim type) from different angles, which is itself a meaningful piece of validation, not just a resolution mechanic.

**Are D7/sufficiency-recognition and D8/search-persistence actually one disposition?** — and **are D12/feasibility-testing and D13/upstream-mapping actually one disposition?** These were pre-registered, in the category-space artifact, as interaction hypotheses (IH1 and IH2) to be tested by cross-referencing what the blind pass independently found under each item's home category, rather than searched for directly. Both get a real, evidence-based answer:

- IH2 (feasibility-testing / upstream-mapping): **yes, tightly coupled**, and the blind pass's own independent description of its master meta-heuristic (`planning-habit-01`: "enumerate the full option space, force adversarial stress-testing, then decide") names both as sequential *components of one ritual*, without ever having been asked whether they were related. This reads as componential rather than identical — distinguishable steps (gather, then test) inside one well-established, higher-order habit — but the coupling is real and strong.
- IH1 (sufficiency-recognition / search-persistence, and per the artifact's phrasing possibly also upstream-mapping): **related, not identical**, and the relationship may genuinely be three-way rather than two-way. Search-persistence branch (a) — knowing when to *stop gathering* — reads, on inspection, almost as the stopping-condition half of upstream-mapping's *gathering* activity; they describe two sides of the same behavior in several of the same evidence instances. Sufficiency-recognition is the same underlying "internal completeness signal" applied to a different target (a finished deliverable or process, not an information-gathering activity). The blind pass, blind to this question, still kept all of them as separate entries with distinguishable evidence throughout nine files — which argues against fully merging them — but their consistent co-occurrence argues for treating them as siblings under one shared disposition (an internal "enough" detector) rather than as fully unrelated traits.

One honest gap: the category-space artifact names a third interaction hypothesis, **IH3**, but the version available doesn't specify what it tests. It cannot be characterized or tested here, and this is flagged as a real information gap rather than guessed at.

## 6. Phase translation results

Harmonizing every process-position note left by the blind pass reveals two structurally different kinds of behavior, not one:

**Cluster A — a single, tight transition point.** Five of the highest-confidence entries in this entire model — the master planning ritual, problem decomposition, feasibility-testing, upstream-mapping, and risk/stakes framing — independently cluster at the *same* transition: the point where assessment, option-enumeration, and stress-testing are finished and he is about to lock in an approach and start building on it. This is not five separate observations that happen to agree; it's the same underlying moment described from five different angles across nine files with no coordination between them.

**Cluster B — recurring checkpoints, not a single point.** A second group — verification timing, gap-checking, and sufficiency recognition — does *not* fit a single "before build" moment. These fire reactively at every "claimed complete" checkpoint (which can occur well after building has started, and recurs multiple times across a project), not once at kickoff.

Checked against P3's current trigger language, sourced entirely from self-report — *"I would prefer claude interupting me before the building phase and inject the implementation part over there before moving to building systems"* — Cluster A **strongly confirms** it. This is a clean, direct convergence between a claim that had zero independent file-behavior evidence when it was made and five independently-derived, high-confidence behavioral findings that land on exactly the same point.

One precision refinement is proposed for the trigger language itself, not a correction: "before the building phase" reads naturally as "before code gets written," but the data shows the operative transition is earlier and broader than that — it's the moment *any* committed execution path is chosen (which includes committing to a document structure, a reference base, or an architecture, not only writing code). Suggested language: **"before commitment to an approach — i.e., once assessment, option-enumeration, and stress-testing are complete but before execution on the chosen approach begins, whether that execution is code, a document, or an architecture."**

Separately, and per the protocol's own instruction to log a trigger point the current policy doesn't encode rather than silently absorb it: **Cluster B needs its own support pattern.** A single pre-build interruption cannot serve verification-timing, gap-checking, or sufficiency-recognition, because those checkpoints recur throughout a project's life, including after building is well underway. This isn't a contradiction of P3 — P3 was never about these three categories — but it is a real, distinct policy need the current single-trigger framing doesn't cover, and is flagged for a decision rather than assumed to already be handled by the existing Interruption Rule.

## 7. Policy layer, final

**Non-negotiable, regardless of any finding:**
- Confidence-tagging and observation/inference separation — never assert an inference as settled fact.
- Falsifiability — every non-trivial claim states what would change it.
- Care-override — absolute, above every trade-off. (See §3, Special Case.)

**Strong default, revisable against how this person actually reacts in practice:**
- **Interruption rule** — only surface something proactively when its value clearly outweighs the cost of disrupting flow. **Trigger language, updated per §6:** *before commitment to an approach* — once assessment, option-enumeration, and stress-testing are complete but before execution on the chosen approach begins (code, document, or architecture alike) — rather than "before the build phase" narrowly read as "before code." **Separately flagged, not yet resolved into a rule:** a second class of behavior (verification-timing, gap-checking, sufficiency-recognition) needs support at recurring "claimed-complete" checkpoints throughout a project, not at one single pre-commitment point — this is a genuine gap in the current single-trigger framing, logged here for a decision rather than folded silently into the rule above.
- Minimal-hint default — prefer a small, incomplete cue over a full explanation, so his own attention does the rest of the work.
- Stopping-support default — track his own stopping signal rather than impose an external one; lean toward encouraging continued exploration over premature closure. Directly reinforced by the search-persistence findings above: his own "gut feeling threshold" repeatedly outlasted an AI's own suggestions to stop.
- Steer, don't seize — treat any intervention as a small nudge to an ongoing process, never a full reset.
- Treat apparent shortcuts as adaptations, not defects, by default — don't "correct" something unless there's actual evidence the underlying tradeoff has failed. (Directly relevant to belief-revision: generic pushback doesn't move him, and shouldn't be read as him being wrong to hold firm.)
- Elicit before importing outside labels — describe him using vocabulary drawn from what was actually found (matching, e.g., the category-space artifact's own discipline of stripping discovered-fact language out of category definitions before they travel).
- Project-continuity scan — when a new problem comes up, check whether it connects to earlier work and surface that connection; this running by default doesn't exempt it from the interruption rule and minimal-hint default — a found connection is a candidate to raise, not automatic license to raise it at length. Directly reinforced by cross-project-continuity-01's promotion: the underlying habit this policy assumes is now one of the best-evidenced findings in the model, so the policy's premise is solid — but its own built-in restraint clause (surface, don't elaborate, unless asked) still applies in full.

## 8. Open questions and caveats

- **Chronology remains incomplete.** File 9 fixed LegacyBridge < TabVolt < NeuraX-VitalNet, but where the other two project domains (the road-safety hackathon thread and the agtech market-research thread) sit relative to this chain is still unresolved.
- **No genuinely two-way, co-decided context has been observed anywhere in either model.** Every project in the corpus is either entirely solo by explicit choice, or has a teammate whose role is cleanly, non-negotiably separated from his own (e.g., "PPT would be dealt with by KK"). This is a real, structural gap in what either model can say about how this person operates when a technical decision is jointly owned rather than individually made and then explained.
- **IH3** is referenced by name in the category-space artifact with no specified content in the version available here — genuinely untestable, not silently dropped.
- **Sensitivity-map result, corrected on review:** of the six categories pre-registered as hardest to catch, five (belief-revision, planning-habit, risk/tradeoff, scope-evolution, and output-quality-under-strain) ended up populated with Established-tier, high-confidence findings that hold up on direct inspection against their category definitions — including output-quality-under-strain, one of the two Low-sensitivity items, which is a clean, boundary-free second instance of genuine adversity. The sixth, curiosity-direction — the *other* Low-sensitivity item — does not: on inspection (§3), its evidence is real and well-evidenced but consistently falls just inside the category's excluded territory (curiosity arising within directed work, not the genuinely undirected exploration the category asked about), and the category as strictly defined remains untested/insufficient-evidence. This is exactly the outcome the sensitivity map predicted for this specific item, for the specific reason it gave (task-directed material dominates the corpus) — so rather than the caution not mattering in practice, as the first version of this report stated, it mattered for precisely the one item the map was most worried about, and that earlier "all six" framing was wrong and is corrected here.
- **Every finding in this report is a qualitative, revisable hypothesis about how one person reasons, not a measured fact.** Confidence levels describe how much independent evidence currently supports a reading, not a probability that the reading is correct. The single strongest form of validation available anywhere in this project — two structurally independent models converging on the same finding without shared access to each other's evidence — appears repeatedly above (gap-checking, upstream-mapping, search-persistence, cross-project-continuity, the phase-translation cluster) and should be weighted accordingly; everywhere else, including every Established item without that specific cross-validation, this remains one read of one person's material, offered for revision the moment they say otherwise.
